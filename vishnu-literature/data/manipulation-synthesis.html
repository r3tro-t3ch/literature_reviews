Beyond picking and placing

The collection spans rigid-object rearrangement, contact assembly, in-hand rotation, cloth handling, mobile and bimanual tasks, and fabrication. These tasks stress different capabilities: semantic interpretation, geometry, feedback speed, friction, compliance and long-horizon recovery. The synthesis below is a review assessment grounded in the linked papers.

A general manipulation reading path

Begin with Transporter Networks, ACT and Diffusion Policy for visual action learning. Continue to OpenVLA, π0 and π0.5 for generalist policies. Compare SayCan and VoxPoser for explicit hierarchy and planning, then follow a task family in the library. Force-aware control and online RL are especially relevant when geometry alone cannot determine the action.

Current research gaps across tasks

Transfer under contact: performance on one insertion, garment or hand does not establish robustness to new friction, compliance or tooling. Recovery over long sequences: local successes must compose with failure detection and retry behavior. Responsive execution: expensive action generators need feedback between predictions; real-time residual learning and fast/slow control explore this. Evidence quality: simulated scores, curated videos and small physical task sets support different claims. New September 2026 preprints extend the reading list, but do not settle these questions.

Start with your project

Measure. Align. Join. Drill.

This review assumes cameras, joint encoders, joint torque/current signals where available, and a wrist force–torque sensor. No tactile skin or fingertip camera is assumed. The proposed workflow below is a review recommendation, not a system already demonstrated by one paper.

01 / MEASURE

Establish the slab

Estimate planes, edges, thickness and a workpiece frame through calibrated contact.

02 / JOIN

Control the fit

Use vision for approach and contact feedback for alignment, insertion and seating.

03 / MACHINE

Verify the hole

Treat feed, deflection, breakthrough and hole quality as a separate process.

What “proprioceptive measurement” can mean

Encoders and calibrated kinematics estimate the probe pose. A contact signal then constrains the slab surface. Joint-torque residuals require a dynamics/friction model; motor current is only an indirect torque estimate. A wrist F/T sensor measures interaction at the tool and is a separate sensing modality. Localizing an unknown contact somewhere on the robot is harder than detecting contact at a known probe tip. Contact Particle Filter and force-based mapping explain those different problems.

For a clamped slab, start with a known probe and fit geometric features to contact points, correcting for tip radius and tool calibration. Compare camera-only, contact-only and fused estimates against an independent reference. Thickness needs access to opposing faces or a calibrated support datum; a top-surface scan alone cannot reveal an unknown underside. Distinguish absolute dimensional error from repeatability. This is a proposed baseline: CLASP supplies a richer learned-shape comparator, but a plausible completed surface is not the same as a measured one.

A reading order that follows the work

  1. Force-based mapping, CLASP and Contact Particle Filter and CoPRE: what contact reveals and what remains ambiguous.
  2. Adaptive admittance for wooden pins, timber-joint RL and demonstrated timber assembly: directly relevant wood-contact control.
  3. ACT and Diffusion Policy, then FoAR and ForceMimic, FACTR and ForceVLA: compare visual action prediction with explicit force use.
  4. Drilling posture mechanics and adaptive timber fabrication: connect control to geometric quality.

Evidence through 29 Sep 2026

What is established—and what to investigate

The useful gap is uncertainty and material transfer across stages.

Integrated timber fabrication already exists: the 2026 screw-press micro-factory coordinates placement, gluing and fastening, while Truss from trash addresses irregular reclaimed material with human involvement. A narrower research question is how calibrated contact measurements and their uncertainty should influence learned joining and drilling across unfamiliar slabs, materials and fixtures. This survey does not establish that complete capability; this is a scoped research inference, not a universal novelty claim.

A concrete research comparison

Hold the robot, tool, wood test pieces, demonstration budget and task tolerances fixed. Compare (1) calibrated geometry plus admittance, (2) vision/proprioception imitation, and (3) the same policy with synchronized wrist F/T. Only then compare force-target prediction, residual RL or a larger pretrained policy. Use held-out slabs and joints, not just held-out camera frames from the same trial. Report measurement uncertainty, completion, peak force, material damage, cycle time and successful recovery separately. These are proposed evaluation criteria, not claimed results.

Methods without conflating them

How the methods fit together

Three lines progressed together: better learned visual action representations, broader cross-robot training, and renewed integration of contact feedback. For woodworking, they solve different parts of the problem; no single leaderboard answers which one meets your dimensional and force requirements.

Force input, force output and force control are distinct. FoAR conditions decisions on measured force/torque. ForceMimic predicts wrench/pose trajectories from point clouds and pose history, then uses force feedback in execution. A successful contact task does not itself show that the neural network received force measurements.

Hierarchy is compatible with learning. A supervisor can sequence probe, align, insert, drill and verify while separate skills perform each stage. “End-to-end” often describes a training mapping, not the removal of inverse kinematics, motion planners or low-level control. A transformer, diffusion head, flow model and mixture of experts are different design axes. ForceVLA explicitly uses a force-aware mixture of experts. That is distinct from simply calling π0’s action branch an “expert”; neither establishes woodworking performance without relevant tests.

Full-order versus reduced-order is not a label to force onto every policy. Torque observers use robot dynamics; contact controllers may use a compact task-space compliance model; perception networks and imitation policies may have no explicit dynamics model at all. The paper cards describe the actual architecture rather than assigning an unsupported model order.
