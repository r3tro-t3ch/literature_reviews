What is actually on hardware?

Pronking means approximately synchronized four-foot takeoff and landing with a flight phase. Bounding alternates fore- and hind-leg pairs. A single jump, repeated pronking, terrain traversal and a full aerial rotation solve different problems; they should not share one performance ranking.

The gap is in the combination

Within this reviewed set, there is strong evidence for individual components. I did not find a verified demonstration combining arbitrary 3D acrobatic maneuvers, precise repeated landings, online terrain understanding, onboard execution and sustained reliability in one system. This is a review inference about the evidence collected here, not a claim that every possible combination is unpublished.

A useful project question

Can a quadruped maintain a commanded sequence of pronks and hop-turns on uncertain terrain while controlling landing error, impact and the ability to take off again?

  1. Landing quality over a sequence. Existing terrain-jumping and landing methods offer strong baselines, but the next launch is a stricter test than merely avoiding a fall. Measure position and orientation error at touchdown, body stabilization time, foot slip, impact, and successful continuation across a predeclared sequence. Compare residual jumping, terrain-aware jumping and terrain-adaptive landing.
  2. Terrain mechanics, not just terrain height. Grass and compliant-robot results mean “jumping on non-rigid systems” is not a new claim by itself. A sharper question is adaptation to independently varied sinkage, friction and stiffness, including changes between consecutive contacts. Compare a rigid baseline and a compliant design under matched actuator limits. Use compliant-template control and ILC as starting points; do not equate leg springs with a model of deformable soil.
  3. Generative planning under a control deadline. Diffusion already plans contacts in simulation, and flow matching already has physical locomotion evidence. The question is whether a generative proposal improves contact choice or landing viability over a strong RL/MPC baseline at equal runtime. Measure missed deadlines, distribution shifts and recovery, not just reference-tracking fidelity. Relevant contrasts: diffusion contact plans, DynaFlow and DIAL-MPC.
  4. Skill transitions with measured reliability. Parkour already selects or combines behaviors, so “a hierarchy of skills” alone is not a gap. Study transitions between pronking, yaw turns, recovery and a selected aerial maneuver. Compare a dense policy, explicit skill hierarchy and sparse experts at matched active compute and training budget. ANYmal Parkour, DiffuseLoco and MoE parkour provide distinct baselines.
  5. Gravity and surface changes together. Low-gravity attitude control is established in simulation and constrained tests. A narrower research target is landing and re-launch under simultaneous uncertainty in gravity, terrain mechanics and contact geometry. Validate gravity emulation separately from soil/contact fidelity, and separate constrained-rig evidence from free 3D motion. Start with SpaceBok limb-based control and SpaceHopper.

What a convincing experiment would report

These are proposed evaluation criteria, not claims from a single paper: use the same robot, payload, power limits, sensing and compute for controller comparisons; separate known from held-out terrain; predeclare trial counts; report all attempts and uncertainty around success rates. Include maximum and typical landing error, peak motor demand, energy per distance, failure type, recovery time and consecutive successful hops. Record safety-rig constraints, human intervention, onboard versus offboard computation and whether external motion capture is used.

Avoid comparing obstacle height to centre-of-mass rise, drop height to takeoff height, or one best trial to repeated-trial success. A 180° backflip ending inverted is not an upright-to-upright 360° flip. Earth and lunar-gravity distances require different interpretations.

Earth or extraterrestrial?

Most reviewed acrobatics targets terrestrial robots. The SpaceBok and SpaceHopper line explicitly targets low-gravity exploration, with validation through simulation and Earth-based rigs. No reviewed source establishes quadruped acrobatics deployed on the Moon, Mars or an asteroid. ETH explicitly says SpaceBok is not space-graded. Regolith interaction, vacuum, thermal conditions and flight-qualified operation therefore remain separate validation questions. ETH platform description · SpaceHopper paper.

Evidence brief / 02

Methods are several different axes.

A controller can be learned, hierarchical and full-body at the same time. Model order describes its dynamics representation; RL describes how a policy is learned; diffusion, flow matching and MoE describe different kinds of models or architectures. “End-to-end” must specify its input and output—it rarely removes every low-level motor controller.

Evidence brief / 03

Where bipeds and quadrupeds meet.

There is shared methodology, but a simple one-way lineage would overstate the evidence. DeepMimic (2018) established a broad simulated motion-imitation approach; animal imitation (2020) brought related ideas to physical quadrupeds. Modern humanoid work such as ASAP addresses simulator mismatch, while BeyondMimic combines motion tracking and generative composition. Their ideas are relevant across morphologies; the inspected evidence does not demonstrate unchanged policies transferring between them.

One direct bridge is OPT-Mimic: the same quadruped platform learns both dynamic quadruped behaviors and biped stepping. This is two-legged behavior on a quadruped, not proof of transfer to a humanoid. In the other direction, ABot-C0 explicitly motivates quadruped learning by the large-scale motion-tracking recipe used in humanoids; its authors identify animal-data scarcity and fragile retargeting as obstacles.

A particularly explicit proposed connection appears in Two-Stage Learning: its authors name humanoid application as future work. That is a research direction, not a completed transfer result.

For your project, transfer the training and control principles first: reference generation, residual physics correction, contact-aware rewards, recovery objectives and motion composition. Treat transfer of weights or motion clips as a separate experiment. Different limb inertia, joint ranges, support geometry and available aerial control require revalidation.
